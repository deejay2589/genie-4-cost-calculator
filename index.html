How Genie 4 pricing works

Genie 4 became available on the Gemini API (Interactions API) on November 19, 2026. It streams a playable 720p world at 24 frames per second with synchronized stereo audio. Google bills by emitted video output tokens, not by wall-clock seconds:

  • 240 video tokens per frame × 24 fps = 5,760 tokens per second.
  • Video output costs $17.50 per 1M tokens (audio included) → 5,760 × $17.50 / 1M = $0.1008 per second, about $6.05 per minute.
  • Text / reference-image input: $1.50 per 1M tokens. Text output: $9.00 per 1M tokens.
  • There is no free tier for Genie 4 — paid tier only. No Batch API, no Flex or Priority inference, no context caching.
  • One session is capped at 120 seconds (about $12.10 of video). Daily session caps apply per tier: 50 / 250 / 1,000.

FAQ

Why is it billed per token instead of per second?

Because the model bills on what it emits: every frame costs a fixed 240 video tokens. At a steady 24 fps that works out to a flat $0.1008/second, but the token basis is what Google actually meters — and it is why a session that stays open keeps charging even if nobody is steering the world.

Does closing the tab stop the billing?

No. Billing runs for as long as the session stays open. You must call complete() on the interaction to end the session and stop the meter. Forgetting this is the most common way a Genie 4 bill surprises people.

Is audio charged separately?

No. The synchronized 48 kHz stereo audio is bundled into the video token rate — $17.50 per 1M video tokens covers both picture and sound.

Can I use Genie 4 for free?

Not through the API: Genie 4 is paid-tier only on the Gemini API, with no free tier. Google AI Ultra subscribers can use it in Project Genie instead, which is a separate subscription path.

How accurate is this calculator?

It uses Google’s published Standard paid-tier rates as of November 19, 2026 ($17.50 / $1.50 / $9.00 per 1M tokens). Actual bills depend on your exact token counts — prompt length, reference images and any text output. Google may change prices; treat this as an estimate, not a quote.
